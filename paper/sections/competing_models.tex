\section{Competing Fatigue Models}\label{sec:competing}

Theorem~\ref{thm:even} converts the empirical fact of positive splitting
into a model-selection problem: some mechanism outside the pure budget makes
late swimming different from early swimming, and different mechanisms leave
different fingerprints on the splits. We formalize five, sharing one
objective, one measured hydrodynamic block, one engine
($R$, $E_0$, and measured $\tau = 16.5$~s), and differing only in the active
mechanism plus a per-model $\phi$ calibrated so every variant reproduces the
same 1:40.0 optimum. Holding the engine fixed is what makes the comparison
about mechanism rather than fitness. The shared cost and constraint family is
\begin{equation}
  C(v, \Erem, x) = k\,v^{3}\Big(1 + \beta_E D + \beta_x \tfrac{x}{L}\Big),
  \qquad
  v \;\le\; v_{\max}\big(1 - \gamma D\big),
  \qquad
  D = 1 - \Erem/E_0 ,
  \label{eq:family}
\end{equation}
with $(\beta_E, \beta_x, \gamma)$ the couplings; setting all three to zero
with $\tau = 0$ recovers the baseline.

\begin{description}
\item[M0 — constant economy.] Nothing changes during the race. Prediction:
exactly even fractions $(0.2500)^{\times 4}$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:even}. As a
description of behaviour it is wrong; as the null it is exactly right.

\item[M1 — oxygen kinetics.] $\tau = 16.5$~s, no fatigue coupling.
Prediction: exactly even, and a slower race
(Proposition~\ref{prop:kinetics}). M1 can never beat M0 on shape; it is
included because ruling out the most intuitive story is informative.

\item[M2 — reserve-coupled cost.] Economy degrades as the tank empties
($\beta_E > 0$): a full reserve is worth protecting for its own sake, so the
optimum holds back early and empties the tank late. Prediction: a
\emph{strong negative split}, fractions $(.277, .259, .240, .224)$ at the
registry coupling. This is sign-falsifiable against any positively split
dataset, which is precisely why it earns its place.

\item[M3 — position-coupled cost.] The same velocity is simply cheaper early
than late ($\beta_x > 0$): declining propelling efficiency, rising body
position cost, accumulating metabolite --- lumped as a linear-in-distance
multiplier. By Theorem~\ref{thm:shape} the optimum is closed-form,
$t_i \propto w_i^{1/3}$: a smooth, near-linear \emph{positive} split,
$(.242, .248, .253, .258)$. Measured lap-by-lap propelling efficiency does
fall significantly across a 200-m swim \citep{figueiredo2011energy}, though
the measured lap energy cost is U-shaped rather than linear, so linearity is
a first approximation and is treated as such.

\item[M4 — fatigue-coupled velocity ceiling.] Positive splitting through a
\emph{constraint} rather than a cost ($\gamma > 0$): the swimmer does not
choose to slow down, they become unable to go fast, the Keller-type
structure \citep{keller1973theory, mathis1989fatigue}. Prediction: a
\emph{front-loaded} positive split with the ceiling binding at the optimum,
$(.240, .249, .254, .257)$.
\end{description}

\subsection{Three shapes, one discriminator}

The five models make three qualitatively distinct predictions: even (M0/M1),
negative (M2), and two kinds of positive (M3/M4). M0 and M1 are
shape-identical by Proposition~\ref{prop:kinetics} and are reported as one
shape hypothesis. M3 and M4 are separated not by aggregate fit but by
\emph{where the fade lives}: M3 predicts an even fade (lap-1$\to$2 drop
$\approx$ lap-3$\to$4 drop, $0.53$ vs $0.49$~s at registry parameters), M4
concentrates the loss between the first two laps and flattens late
($0.99$ vs $0.24$~s, a ratio near four). The per-race statistic
$\Delta_{12}/\Delta_{34}$ is therefore pre-registered as the discriminating
secondary outcome; it is sharper than RMSE because it is a shape ratio rather
than a magnitude.

\subsection{What each model would need to be believed}

M0 wins if observed pacing is even after start correction. M1 cannot win on
shape and no shape claim is made for it. M2 wins only if corrected pacing is
negatively split. M3 wins if the fade is near-linear; M4 if it is
front-loaded with a flat finish. And for either positive-split model, a
fitted coupling is believable only when it is not standing in for the
un-modelled dive start: the start credit and both couplings push lap~1 the
same way, a confound quantified in Section~\ref{sec:results-empirical} and
governed by the pre-registered start-uncertainty clause of the validation
plan.
