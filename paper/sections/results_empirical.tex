\section{Empirical Results}\label{sec:results-empirical}

All results in this section come from the frozen pilot-v0.2 dataset under
the plan of Section~\ref{sec:methods}. Calibration used the 256 training
races only; the 89 held-out races (60 swimmers, ten meets, final times
1:39.6--2:25.2) were opened once, by one script, on 3~September~2026, after
every fitted value had been written to disk.

\subsection{What the races look like}

Across all 345 usable races the mean free-swimming-equivalent shares at the
registered 1.80~s credit are $(24.17, 24.79, 25.57, 25.47)\%$: a positive
split, with 91\% of races having $P_4>P_1$. Two features of the profile are
worth stating before any model is scored. The fade is front-loaded --- the
mean lap slows by 0.76~s from lap~1 (credited) to lap~2 and by 0.92~s from
lap~2 to lap~3 --- and then it stops: the fourth lap is on average
0.13~s \emph{faster} than the third, and in 52\% of races the last 50 is
the faster of the two. The third lap is the slowest lap in 48\% of races.
No model in the family of Section~\ref{sec:competing} produces a
non-monotone profile, because every mechanism in it is monotone in position
or reserve; the finishing kick is therefore outside the model class by
construction, and we return to it in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Registered calibration}

Table~\ref{tab:fits} gives the training-side fits. The position-coupled
model's $\beta_x$ moved from the registry guess of 0.28 to 0.228, improving
the training loss from 0.472 to 0.465 percentage points; the
velocity-ceiling coupling $\gamma$ moved from 0.18 to 0.269 (0.472 to
0.461~pp); and the reserve-coupled $\beta_E$ was driven to its lower bound
of 0.02, where M2 is nearly even and still worse than M0, which is exactly
how a sign-contradicted mechanism should behave under a fit. The fitted
shapes are $(24.35, 24.79, 25.22, 25.63)\%$ for M3 and
$(24.07, 25.04, 25.37, 25.51)\%$ for M4. Two remarks. First, the
improvements from fitting are in the third decimal of a percentage point:
the registry shapes were already near the dispersion floor of the data, and
most of every model's loss is race-to-race scatter, not misplacement of the
mean. Second, during this calibration the inner optimal-control solve for
M4 was found to return a quiet local optimum from a single restart; the
registered fitter therefore uses at least five restarts and records, for
every ODE fit, that it reproduces the independently computed registry shape
(to 0.004~pp here). The exploratory pilot fits predate that fix and are
labelled accordingly.

\subsection{Held-out comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:holdout} and Figure~\ref{fig:holdout} give the registered
comparison. The positive-split models are decisively more accurate than the
alternatives: M3's held-out mean RMSE is 0.454~pp [0.398, 0.523] and M4's
0.461~pp [0.415, 0.515], against 0.653~pp [0.568, 0.759] for the even
models and 0.771~pp [0.682, 0.880] for M2. The paired differences are
unambiguous --- the even models are worse than M3 by 0.200~pp
[0.144, 0.262] and M2 by 0.317~pp [0.256, 0.385], both intervals far from
zero --- so the pre-registered criterion rejects the even class and the
negative-split class. Between M3 and M4 the data have nothing to say: the
difference is 0.007~pp with a 95\% interval of $[-0.019, +0.030]$, M3 is
the better model in 30 held-out races and M4 in 37, and on shape both
predict the observed sign of $P_4-P_1$. Applying Section~\ref{sec:methods}
mechanically, no single model wins on accuracy and no single model wins on
shape; the registered conclusion is that \emph{the data distinguish the
shape classes but not the two positive-split mechanisms}. The
published-age stratum (73 held-out races, club-linked rows removed) gives
the same picture, M4 at 0.463~pp and M3 at 0.469~pp, both far below M0 at
0.706~pp.

The pre-registered discriminator explains why M3 and M4 tie. The held-out
drop from lap~1 to lap~2 is $+0.75$~s [0.56, 0.99] and from lap~3 to lap~4
is $-0.03$~s [$-0.18$, $+0.10$]. At the held-out mean race duration the
fitted M3 predicts drops of 0.51 and 0.47~s (an even fade) and the fitted
M4 predicts 1.11 and 0.16~s (a front-loaded fade). The observed pattern is
M4's in kind --- large early loss, flat finish --- but M4 overshoots the
early drop by as much as M3 undershoots it, and neither predicts a final
lap faster than the third. The per-lap signed errors say the same thing:
the mean held-out shares are $(24.15, 24.76, 25.56, 25.53)\%$, so M3 puts
0.20 points too much time in lap~1 and 0.34 too little in lap~3, while M4
gets laps~1 and~4 within 0.1 points but puts 0.28 too much in lap~2 and
0.19 too little in lap~3. Aggregate RMSE, which sums these, cannot prefer
one, and the discriminating statistic points to a profile between them with
a feature outside both.

\subsection{The start credit decides the ranking}

Figure~\ref{fig:band} and Table~\ref{tab:band} sweep the credit applied to
the held-out data across the registered band while every fitted parameter
stays at its 1.80~s training value. The ranking changes twice inside the
band: M4 is the most accurate model for credits of 1.2--1.6~s, M3 for
1.8--2.6~s, and the even models for 2.8--3.4~s; at 3.4~s the observed mean
profile itself becomes a negative split. Under item~3 of the plan's
start-uncertainty clause, the registered conclusion is that the data cannot
distinguish the surviving models given the uncertainty in the start credit.
The upper part of the band is not an academic extreme: it is where the
measured elite 15~m start times land when converted to this field's
race pace. Refitting $\beta_x$ at each credit on the training side (a
supplementary analysis, closed-form and therefore cheap) shows the same
confound from the parameter's side: the fitted coupling falls from 0.334 at
1.2~s to 0.010 at 3.4~s, roughly 0.15 of $\beta_x$ per second of assumed
credit, and its held-out advantage over the even model shrinks from
0.34~pp to 0.002~pp across the band. Position fatigue and the dive are, at
this population's pace, nearly the same thing to a split sheet.

\subsection{Deviation and performance: a null}

The registered regression (Table~\ref{tab:h1}, Figure~\ref{fig:h1}) uses
the 110 races by 71 swimmers with a pre-race personal best. The random
intercept per swimmer was identifiable in every fit, so the mixed model was
used throughout. With deviation measured from the fitted M3 optimum the
linear coefficient is $b_1=+0.38$~\% per point with a 95\% interval of
$[-4.09, +4.86]$; from the M4 optimum, $+1.04$ $[-3.72, +5.80]$; from the
even optimum, $-1.46$ $[-4.62, +1.69]$. No interval excludes zero, in
either direction, for any model. The primary hypothesis is not supported,
and per the plan this is reported as a null, not as an absence of evidence
to be argued around.

The registered fit also returned a significantly positive curvature for M3
and M4 ($b_2=+14.7$ $[+6.3, +23.2]$ and $+19.9$ $[+9.5, +30.3]$), which
would say that large deviations go with large improvements. Inspection
showed what it was: the three largest ``improvements'' in the set (29\%,
14\% and 13\%) were measured against personal bests swum four years earlier
at ages 13--14, which are not expectations in any useful sense. A post-hoc
supplement --- exploratory, and reported as such --- restricting the
personal best to one set within the previous 365 days (85 races, 65
swimmers) removes the curvature entirely ($b_2=+0.4$ $[-8.8, +9.6]$ for
M3) and leaves the linear term null ($b_1=+2.6$ $[-1.3, +6.6]$). The
lesson is about the expectation model rather than about pacing, and it is
taken up in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Two descriptive observations, not registered}

Two patterns in the full usable set are worth recording for the next
study, with the caveat that neither was pre-registered. First, faster
swimmers pace more evenly: the fastest tercile by final time has mean
corrected shares of $24.61\%$ on lap~1 and $25.32\%$ on lap~4 (a spread of
0.7 points), the slowest tercile $23.71\%$ and $25.64\%$ (1.9 points).
This is the direction the theory predicts if pacing skill is part of what
makes a swimmer fast, but a constant 1.80~s credit under-corrects slower
swimmers, whose dive is worth more seconds at their pace, so part of the
pattern is the start again. Second, swims at the high-school championships are slightly more
evenly paced than swims at club invitationals (lap-1 share 24.6\% against
24.1\%), consistent with the same reading, since the championship fields
are faster, or with a difference in how a heat and a final are raced.
