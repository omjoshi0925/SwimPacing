\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Sources and provenance}

The empirical material is official meet results: Hy-Tek Meet Manager
result sheets published by the host clubs and by Pacific Swimming for
USA~Swimming sanctioned meets in Northern California, and the official
result sheets of the North Coast Section and CIF State high-school
championships. Every source publishes cumulative splits at 50, 100, 150 and
200 yards for each completed swim, together with the swimmer's name, age
(club meets) or grade (high-school meets), team, seed time and final time.
Nothing was measured by us; the dataset is a verified transcription of
public records.

Sources were admitted at one of two provenance tiers, decided per file and
recorded in the repository's dataset register. \emph{Tier~1, checksum-verified
retrieval}: the event section was extracted from the official page by a
scripted browser procedure that recomputes length and hash checks on the
saved text, and the file's SHA-256 digest is published so that anyone
re-retrieving the official page can confirm the dataset was built from the
genuine file. \emph{Tier~2, verified transcription}: for five meets a
third-party extraction workbook was supplied by the project owner; each meet
was admitted only after the official file was identified and its rows were
independently spot-verified against it. That check was not a formality: one
workbook meet was contradicted by its official file and was excluded, and in
another the third and fourth laps appeared transposed, so only its final
times were kept. Sixteen meets swum between October~2021 and July~2026
contributed 1{,}382 rows in total; five of them (three SwimCloud final-time
listings, one meet whose official sheet prints no splits, and the
transposed-lap meet) contribute final times only and serve the personal-best
history rather than the shape analysis.

Every row passes through one parser that refuses, rather than guesses at,
anything it does not recognize: the printed lap times are checked against
the cumulative splits, the last cumulative split must equal the final time,
and an entry with a partial split set is reported as a problem in every
mode. Swimmers are anonymized at ingest to identifiers of the form S\#\#\#
through a single private identity map that is never published; cross-meet
identity is matched on normalized name, and, because two different people
shared a name in the expanded data, a published age that is impossible for
the matched identity (by birthday-counting bounds across the date gap) now
forces a separate identity. No name, and no file containing names, enters
the public repository.

\subsection{Population rule and the club-linked age path}

The target population is male swimmers aged 15--18 racing the 200-yard
freestyle in a short-course-yards pool. Club meets publish a point age, so
the rule is applied directly. High-school championships publish grades,
which we never use to infer age. Instead, under an amendment to the
analysis plan approved by the project owner before the dataset was frozen
and before the test set was opened, a grade-only row enters the population
only when the same swimmer has age-published rows at other meets and every
age consistent with those rows across the date gap lies inside 15--18. No
point age is invented, contradictory links derive nothing and are flagged,
and a field \texttt{age\_source} marks each admitted row as
\texttt{published} or \texttt{club\_linked} so every result can be re-run on
the published-only stratum (Section~\ref{sec:results-empirical} does so).
At adoption the audit found zero conflicting links.

\subsection{The frozen dataset}

Table~\ref{tab:funnel} gives the pre-registered exclusion funnel. Of the
832 rows with complete cumulative splits, none failed the monotonicity,
final-time agreement ($|S_{200}-T|\le 0.05$~s), implausible-lap or duplicate
checks; every exclusion after the split requirement is the population rule.
The frozen dataset, \texttt{pilot-v0.2}, holds \textbf{345 usable races} by
248 swimmers across eleven meets (seven club invitationals, three
high-school championships, one intrasquad meet): 268 timed finals, 57
preliminary swims and 20 finals. Ages are published for 291 races (15:~91,
16:~110, 17:~71, 18:~19) and club-linked for 54. Final times average
1:53.2 (SD 9.7~s, range 1:37.0--2:40.0), a broad age-group field rather
than an elite one. Sixty swimmers have more than one usable race (157 races
in all), and 110 races by 71 swimmers have a pre-race personal best derived
from an earlier row of the same identity within the dataset; no personal
best is imported from outside it. No source publishes 15~m start times.

The dataset is frozen by digest: the raw and processed files' SHA-256 values
are recorded in the dataset register together with the per-meet counts and
provenance tiers, and any later change requires a new version entry. The
earlier 80-race pilot (\texttt{pilot-v0.1}, one meet, January 2025) is
preserved unchanged as the exploratory baseline discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:methods}.

\subsection{Known biases}

All eleven meets are in one region (Northern California), so the sample
reflects one competitive culture and a small set of pools. The two largest
contributors are senior-open invitationals whose 15--18 entrants are
committed club swimmers; the high-school championship rows are, by
selection, the region's faster swimmers. Preliminary swims (57 races) may be
paced to qualify rather than to minimize time, a point taken up in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. Personal bests are derived only from races in
the dataset, so ``improvement on personal best'' is improvement on the
swimmer's best \emph{recorded here}, which for a developing 15-year-old with
one earlier row is a weak proxy for expectation.
