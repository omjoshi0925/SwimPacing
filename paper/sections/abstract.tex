\begin{abstract}
What pacing strategy minimizes 200-yard freestyle race time, and do
competitive swimmers use it? We formulate the four-lap race as a constrained
optimization problem over split velocities, with a cost function derived from
measured hydrodynamic drag, a two-parameter aerobic--anaerobic energy system,
and oxygen-uptake kinetics, and we prove two structural results. First, under
a fixed energy budget with velocity-only cost, even pacing is the unique
optimum --- a theorem, holding for every cost exponent $p>1$. Second, the
optimal pacing \emph{shape} is invariant to aerobic capacity, anaerobic
capacity, drag, and efficiency: those parameters set how fast the race is
swum, and provably not how it is divided. Because real 200-yard races are not
evenly paced, we formalize five competing fatigue mechanisms (constant
economy, oxygen kinetics, reserve-coupled cost, position-coupled cost, and a
fatigue-coupled velocity ceiling) that make three qualitatively distinct,
falsifiable predictions about split shape, and we test them against 345
anonymized official races by male swimmers aged 15--18 from eleven meets,
under a pre-registered plan with one fitted parameter per model and a
swimmer-grouped held-out set opened once. Held-out accuracy rejects the
even and negative-split mechanisms (mean split-share RMSE 0.65 and 0.77
percentage points against 0.45--0.46 for the positive-split models, with
bootstrap intervals that exclude zero difference), but cannot separate the
two positive-split mechanisms: the observed fade is front-loaded in kind
and between them in degree, and the ranking among surviving models changes
across the literature-anchored band of dive-start credits, so by the
registered criterion the data cannot distinguish them given start
uncertainty. The pre-registered test of whether swimmers nearer the
predicted optimum perform better relative to expectation returns the null
the plan anticipated, and the mean profile shows a finishing kick that no
monotone fatigue mechanism can produce. The pacing penalties the model
predicts are quadratic and small near the optimum, robust to parameter
uncertainty, and an order of magnitude below race-to-race noise; the start
correction, rather than the fatigue physics, is the decisive open quantity.
\end{abstract}
