\section{Structural Results}\label{sec:theorems}

Three results organize everything that follows. The first two are proved in
split-time coordinates, where the problem's convexity is visible; the third
is a two-line consequence of the supply dynamics. All three are verified
numerically in the repository against three independent solvers (closed form,
sequential quadratic programming in split-time space, and direct integration
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dynamics}), which agree to about $10^{-11}$~s.

\subsection{Even pacing is a theorem, not a finding}

Work in split times $t_i = d/v_i$. The objective
Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} becomes linear, $T = \sum_i t_i$, and with
instantaneous supply ($\tau=0$) the terminal energy budget is
\begin{equation}
  B(t) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{4} C\!\left(\frac{d}{t_i}\right) t_i
       \;=\; k\, d^{\,p} \sum_{i=1}^{4} t_i^{\,1-p}
       \;\le\; R\,T + E_0 .
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Even pacing]\label{thm:even}
For every $p > 1$ and every $k, R, E_0 > 0$, the minimizer of $T$ subject to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget} and box bounds on $t_i$ has $t_1 = t_2 = t_3 = t_4$
whenever the bounds are slack, and it is unique.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $p>1$ each term $t_i^{\,1-p}$ is strictly convex and decreasing on
$t_i>0$, so $g(t) = k d^p \sum_i t_i^{1-p} - R\sum_i t_i - E_0$ is strictly
convex, and the feasible set $\{g \le 0\}$ is convex; minimizing the linear
objective $\sum_i t_i$ over it is a convex program, so the
Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions are necessary and sufficient. The budget is
active at any optimum (otherwise every $t_i$ could be reduced), so with
multiplier $\mu > 0$ stationarity reads, for each $i$,
\[
  1 \;=\; \mu\Big( (p-1)\,k\,d^{\,p}\, t_i^{-p} + R \Big).
\]
The bracket is strictly decreasing in $t_i$, hence invertible, and its value
is the same for every $i$; therefore all $t_i$ are equal. Uniqueness follows
from strict convexity of $g$ on the active budget.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:even} is the reason a model of this class cannot explain
observed positive splitting by appeal to fitness, drag, or reserve size:
under a pure budget, \emph{any} departure from even pacing wastes time, for
every parameter value. Real 200-yard races are positively split, so something
outside the budget structure is at work, and Section~\ref{sec:competing}
formalizes the candidates.

\subsection{The optimal shape does not depend on the engine or the hull}

Let economy decay with position: $C(v, x) = k v^p \,(1 + \beta_x\, x/L)$,
the simplest mechanism that makes late metres dearer than early ones.
Integrating the multiplier over lap $i$ gives the \emph{exact} per-lap weight
$w_i = 1 + \beta_x\, \bar{x}_i / L$ with $\bar x_i$ the lap midpoint (the
multiplier is linear in $x$, so midpoint weighting is exact, not a
quadrature approximation).

\begin{theorem}[Shape invariance]\label{thm:shape}
With position-coupled economy the optimum of the program above satisfies
\begin{equation}
  t_i \;\propto\; w_i^{1/p},
  \qquad w_i = 1 + \beta_x \frac{\bar x_i}{L},
  \label{eq:shape}
\end{equation}
so the optimal split \emph{fractions} $t_i / T$ depend only on $\beta_x$ and
$p$. They are independent of $k$, $R$, $E_0$, $\rho$, $C_D$, $A$, $\eta_p$,
$\eta_g$, and $\phi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The budget becomes $k d^p \sum_i w_i\, t_i^{1-p} \le R\,T + E_0$; the same
KKT computation as in Theorem~\ref{thm:even} gives
$1 = \mu\big((p-1) k d^p w_i t_i^{-p} + R\big)$, hence
$w_i\, t_i^{-p}$ is constant across laps, which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:shape}.
The constant of proportionality (and thus $T$) depends on all parameters;
the fractions do not.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:engine}
Within this model class, a swimmer with greater aerobic capacity, a larger
anaerobic reserve, less drag, or better efficiency should race the
\emph{same} pacing shape, faster. Only the fatigue coupling itself moves the
optimal shape.
\end{corollary}

Numerically, sweeping every non-fatigue parameter over its plausible range
moves the optimal fractions by less than $10^{-7}$ (solver noise), while
$\beta_x$ moves them by percentage points. Corollary~\ref{cor:engine} is the
model's most counterintuitive export to coaching: ``you have a big engine, so
take it out faster'' has no support here.

\subsection{Oxygen kinetics slow the race without bending the shape}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kinetics}
With supply $R(t) = R(1-e^{-t/\tau})$ and terminal-budget accounting, total
aerobic energy over a race of duration $T$ is
$R\big[T - \tau(1 - e^{-T/\tau})\big]$, a function of $T$ alone. Two
velocity profiles finishing in the same time receive identical aerobic
energy, so the optimal split fractions are unchanged for every $\tau$: the
optimum is exactly as in Theorems~\ref{thm:even} and~\ref{thm:shape}, swum
slower.
\end{proposition}

This is a useful negative result. ``Go out fast because the aerobic system
is not up to speed yet'' is the most intuitive physiological story for
front-loading, and within this model it is not a reason to front-load at
all. (With a sufficiently small reserve and slow kinetics the path
constraint $\Erem \ge 0$ can bind and shade the optimum off even; at the
calibrated reserve this does not occur for any $\tau$ up to 200~s, a
boundary the repository documents together with the retraction of an earlier
claim about it.)
